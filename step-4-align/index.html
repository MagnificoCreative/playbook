Agree on What Matters

Share findings before you design, then choose a few measures and let the rest go.

Findings first

By now you understand the problem better than almost anyone in your organization. Resist the urge to start designing.

A dashboard designed before people agree on the problem becomes one more opinion in the room. Findings shared first become common ground, and common ground is what lets you make the hard call this step requires: choosing a few measures and letting the rest go.

How to apply it

01 / 05
  1. Themes

    Group what you heard into a few themes.

    Trust, focus, consistency and adoption are a good starting frame.

  2. Readout

    Hold a readout before any design work.

    Bring leaders, users and IT together, walk through the findings, and agree on the top priorities in the room.

  3. Questions

    Test every candidate measure against the same questions.

    Does it change a decision? Do several roles rely on it? Does the data exist? Can it be trusted? Does it matter to the business?

  4. Categories

    Group what survives into a handful of categories.

    Give each measure one written definition and one owner.

  5. Let go

    Decide what you’ll stop reporting.

    Choosing what matters means choosing what doesn’t.

In the field

At the oil and gas company, the findings came down to four needs, and from those came six opportunities.

Simplifying the metrics stood out as high value for relatively little effort; standardizing and reducing duplication took more cross-functional work but were essential for anything to last.

Needs

Opportunities

Four needs

  • Trust in the data
  • Focus on what mattered
  • Consistency across systems
  • Adoption of tools that fit real work

Six opportunities

  • Simplify the metrics
  • Design for roles
  • Connect context
  • Standardize the data
  • Reduce duplication
  • Enable adoption

From many measures to a few

The platform was ultimately built around five categories: Safety, Quality, Delivery, Cost and People (SQDCP), each with a small set of supporting metrics. SQDCP fit this operator. Your categories may differ. What carries over is the discipline: few categories, chosen on purpose, with every measure defined once and owned by someone.
