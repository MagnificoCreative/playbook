The Dashboard Isn’t the Problem

Why another dashboard rarely fixes operational data, and the six steps that do.

Scroll

You know this meeting

If you lead operations at an energy company, you probably know this meeting. The numbers are on the screen, and nobody agrees on them.

One team’s figure doesn’t match another’s. Someone has a spreadsheet that tells a different story. Most of the hour goes to reconciling the numbers, and very little goes to deciding what to do about them.

The usual response is to ask for a better dashboard: one screen, all the data, finally in one place. It’s a reasonable request, and it rarely works. Not because the dashboard is badly built, but because the problem it’s meant to solve sits underneath it.

Where trust is lost

Operational information loses trust on its way from source to decision. It’s born in sensors, records and systems. It’s exported and reshaped in spreadsheets. It’s reconciled by hand before meetings. It’s presented in dashboards built by different teams. By the time it reaches a decision, nobody is sure which version to believe.

One screen isn’t one truth

That’s the distinction at the heart of this playbook.

You can have the first without the second, and then a new dashboard simply shows the disagreement more efficiently.

How this playbook works

The playbook follows Magnifico’s CLEAR discovery process (Context, Listen, Examine, Align, Roadmap) and adds a final step for building with confidence. Six steps, one chapter each. Every chapter explains the step, shows you how to apply it in your own organization, and ends with an example from our work with an oil and gas company that went through the same process, from the first interviews to a delivered platform.
