One number, many copies

Five ways to show the pattern from the section: data copied between systems and spreadsheets, definitions shifting, figures reconciled by hand, and new dashboards arriving without the old ones leaving. Each repeat costs a little more trust. There are no figures, just shape, drift and fading. Each one is pinned and scrubbed by scroll, and plays backwards when you scroll up.

Option A

A copy of a copy

The number starts as a crisp little chart at the source. Every step copies it to the next frame, and every copy is a bit worse than the last: the line wobbles, a misprinted ghost appears beside it, and the stroke starts to break up. By the end of the row it's still the "same" number, but nobody could swear to it.

Option B

Copies of copies

One source branches into systems, the systems into spreadsheets, and the spreadsheets into more copies. Each dot is the same number, but the dots drift in size the further they get from the source. Then dashed stitches appear where people reconcile neighbours by hand, and new dashboards keep arriving on the right without any of the old ones leaving. Meanwhile the lines back to the source fade.

Option C

Drift and reconcile

Four copies leave the source together and slowly drift apart as definitions shift. Someone pulls them back together by hand, but never quite all the way, and then they drift again, faster. The cycle repeats. The trust line along the bottom breaks up a little more after every round.

Option D

Band-aids on the foundation

This takes the interview quote literally: “the foundation is not solid, so everything on top of it is just trying to band-aid.” Systems, spreadsheets, reports and dashboards drop onto the source one by one. Every joint gets a band-aid where someone reconciled it by hand, the stack leans a little further with each new layer, and cracks spread out from under the source.

Option E

The loop

The process is a loop: copied, redefined, reconciled by hand, reported, and round again. A dot laps the loop as you scroll. Every pass leaves another stray copy at each station, and chips another piece off the ring of trust around the source. After three laps there's plenty of data and very little ring left.
