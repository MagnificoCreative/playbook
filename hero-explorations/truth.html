One screen isn’t one truth

Four ways to show the difference between putting all the data on one screen and agreeing on what it means. Each one is pinned and scrubbed by scroll, and plays backwards when you scroll up.

Option A · Your idea

The spectrum

A scale runs from pane of glass to source of truth, with a marker moving along it as you scroll. Above it, the dots on a crowded screen leave one by one and settle in a ring around the pink ball, each tied to it by a thread. You end with the same data, but it's now all connected to one source.

Option A2 · Ordered screen

The spectrum, from a real dashboard

The same move, but the pane of glass now reads as a dashboard. The dots sit in four faint widgets: a KPI grid, a line chart, a bar chart and a table. They leave in a scattered order, so the whole screen empties at once instead of one widget at a time. The empty widget frames stay behind.

Option A3 · No card

The spectrum, on the page

A2 with no white card. The illustration sits straight on the light gray page with no frame or edge, so it reads as part of the section rather than a box inside it.

Option B · Vertical cut

Under the glass

The screen stays exactly the same the whole time. Underneath it, every tile is wired to a different source, a tangle of spreadsheets and systems. As you scroll, the wires reroute one by one to a single source. The point is that the screen was never the problem.

Option C · A chart

Three answers, one screen

This one takes the block note literally: a new dashboard "simply shows the disagreement more efficiently." One chart shows three teams' versions of the same metric, drawn neatly side by side and disagreeing. Keep scrolling and they pull together into one line.

Option D · Two columns

Line up the panes

Every team already has its own pane of glass, each with its own scatter of data. As you scroll, the panes float into one stack and their dots move into the same places, until one pink thread runs straight through every layer. It's the stacked echo look from the heroes, and it sits beside the copy.

One screen isn’t one truth

A single pane of glass puts all your data on one screen. A single source of truth means everyone agrees what each number means, where it comes from and who owns it.

You can have the first without the second, and then a new dashboard simply shows the disagreement more efficiently.
