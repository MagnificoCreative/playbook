Step 3 · One number, many copies

One number, many copies

Five ways to show the same idea, with no made-up figures. A number is drawn as a small grid of dots. Each copy of it keeps a slightly different set: a dot goes missing here, half a dot there. The paths are dotted, as in the other steps, and pink marks only what moves and what's agreed.

Option 1

Dots for the number

Your idea, on the current layout. The source holds a full set of dots. Its copies split off along three dotted paths, and each copy keeps a slightly different set. Further down the line, a second copy loses a little more. The paths only meet again where a definition is agreed, and that set is whole again.

1 · Dots for the number

Option 2

A copy of a copy

One line, one copy after another. Each step is named for what happens to the number: it's copied, re-keyed, adjusted, reconciled by hand. A little is lost at every step. Then the line is traced back to its source, an owner is named there, and every copy fills back in.

2 · A copy of a copy

Option 3

Drift and reconcile

The source is a dotted line across the card. Three copies start on it and slowly drift away as their definitions shift. Twice, someone pulls them back by hand, and twice they drift again. Only an agreed definition holds them on the line for good.

3 · Drift and reconcile

Option 4

Nothing retired

New tools keep arriving, and each takes its own copy of the number. None of the old ones ever leave, so the copies pile up, each a little different. Then all but one are retired, and the one that's left has an owner.

4 · Nothing retired

Option 5

One screen

The three copies are brought together on one screen. Side by side, the gaps between them are easy to see, and they're marked in pink, but nothing is fixed. A dot then travels back to the source, an owner is named where the data is born, and the copies on the screen fill in to match.
