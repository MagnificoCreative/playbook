Step 5 · What goes where

Lighter than a pyramid

Five quieter ways to show the three tiers: always visible, available on demand, and revealed during investigation. The point is that the most important tier is the smallest one. Everything uses 1px lines, small dots, and the small type from the other charts: 15px for tier names and 14px or 12px for the notes. Each one builds as you scroll.

A · Rings B · Layers C · Density D · Depth E · Type only

Option A

Rings

The first tier is the pink dot at the center: small and always in view. On demand is the ring around it. Investigation is the faint dashed ring at the edge, the largest and furthest away. The most important tier takes up the least room.

Option B

Layers

Three hairline planes stacked like screens, starting as one. As you scroll they separate. The top layer is small and outlined in pink, and each layer underneath is wider and fainter. Depth stands in for “how far you have to go to see it”.

Option C

Density

The information itself, as dots. At first only a handful of pink ones show: the first tier. Scroll and the on-demand dots fill in around them, then the rest of the field appears faintly for investigation. The key below lights up each tier as it arrives.

Option D

Depth

A single line going down, in the same language as the signal-to-action rail just above it on the page. Each tier sits at its own depth, with a row of short ticks for how much information lives there: a few at the surface, more below, and many at the bottom.

Option E

Type only

No illustration at all. The tiers are the type: the first is large and dark with a pink dot, the next is smaller and lighter, and the last is smallest and faintest. The notes are 12px. It's the quietest option, and it makes the point that important doesn't mean equal space on its own.
