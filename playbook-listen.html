Start with people

Most data initiatives start with the data: an inventory of systems, a list of KPIs, a request for requirements. Start with people instead.

Fragmented data is rarely a technology problem at its root. It grows out of how people work, which tools they trust, what they’ve built to get around gaps, and what the organization rewards. None of that shows up on a system diagram. You find it by asking.

How to apply it

01 / 04
  1. Map

    Map who to talk to.

    List every function that creates, interprets or acts on operational data: operations leadership, production, maintenance and reliability, integrity, health and safety, projects, and technology. Include frontline people as well as managers, and always include IT.

  2. Ask

    Ask about work, not wish lists.

    Don’t ask what dashboard people want. Ask them to walk you through a recent decision: which tools they opened, which numbers they checked by hand, where they keep their own records, and why.

  3. Evidence

    Treat workarounds as evidence.

    The spreadsheet someone trusts more than the official system is the most useful thing you’ll find. Note every one.

  4. Patterns

    Look for the pattern behind the symptoms.

    Ask what in your culture, incentives or habits lets fragmentation grow. That is what will have to change, and no tool will change it for you.

In the field

Fragmentation sources

The most important finding wasn’t technical. The company had a strong independent spirit: teams were trusted to solve their own problems their own way. It was a genuine strength. It also meant every team built its own reports, dashboards and naming conventions, and the result was many competing versions of the truth.

The eight sources of fragmentation, sorted into three groups.

Information & Trust

  • Source data quality
  • Replication and duplication
  • Missing shared standards
  • No trusted central data store

Tools & Workflows

  • No system retirement policy
  • Dashboard accessibility

People & Support

  • Data literacy and training
  • IT coordination and capacity
